His Story

A Life Dedicated to the Nation

01

Humble Beginnings

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born into a modest Tamil Muslim family on the island town of Rameswaram. His father, Jainulabdeen, was a boat owner and imam of a local mosque, while his mother Ashiamma was a homemaker. Growing up, young Kalam distributed newspapers to financially support his family's meagre income. Despite the hardships, he was a bright and curious student who was deeply fascinated by mathematics and physics. He graduated from Saint Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, and later pursued aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology — a decision that would change the trajectory of both his life and India's scientific future.

02

The Rocket Scientist

After joining the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) in 1960, Kalam transferred to the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) where he served as the project director for India's first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III), which successfully deployed the Rohini satellite into orbit in July 1980. He then returned to DRDO and led India's Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), overseeing the development of the Agni and Prithvi missiles. His pivotal role in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests in 1998 earned him the enduring title "Missile Man of India."

03

The People's President

In 2002, A.P.J. Abdul Kalam was elected as the 11th President of the Republic of India with overwhelming support from both ruling and opposition parties — a rare feat in Indian politics. During his presidency from 2002 to 2007, he earned the affectionate title "People's President" for his accessibility, warmth, and genuine connection with the common citizen. Unlike many who held the office, Kalam actively engaged with students across the country, visiting schools and colleges, inspiring young minds to dream big and pursue science and innovation.

04

Legacy & Eternal Inspiration

Even after his presidency, Dr. Kalam never retired from public life. He became a visiting professor at IIM Shillong, IIM Ahmedabad, and IIM Indore, and continued his mission of inspiring the youth. He authored several bestselling books, including "Wings of Fire," "Ignited Minds," and "India 2020," which outlined his vision for transforming India into a developed nation. On 27 July 2015, while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, Dr. Kalam collapsed and passed away from a cardiac arrest at the age of 83. He died doing what he loved most — teaching and inspiring the next generation. His legacy continues to ignite the dreams of millions of young Indians.

Milestones

Key Achievements & Timeline

1931

Born in Rameswaram

Born on 15 October in a Tamil Muslim family on the island of Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.

1960

Joins DRDO

After graduating from MIT Chennai, he joined the Aeronautical Development Establishment of DRDO as a scientist.

1969

Transferred to ISRO

Moved to ISRO and became the project director for India's first Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III).

1980

SLV-III Success

Successfully deployed the Rohini satellite into near-earth orbit — India's first indigenous satellite launch.

1981

Padma Bhushan

Awarded the Padma Bhushan, India's third-highest civilian honour, for his contributions to science.

1990

Padma Vibhushan

Received the Padma Vibhushan for his exceptional and distinguished service in the field of science and engineering.

1997

Bharat Ratna

Bestowed the Bharat Ratna — India's highest civilian award — for his immense contributions to Indian science and missile technology.

1998

Pokhran-II Nuclear Tests

Played a pivotal organisational, political, and technical role in India's Pokhran-II nuclear tests, solidifying India's nuclear deterrence.

2002

11th President of India

Elected the 11th President with overwhelming support, widely known as the "People's President" for his humble and accessible nature.

2015

A Teacher Till the End
